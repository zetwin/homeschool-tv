# Colombia Lesson 2: why it's cold near the Equator (fact sheet)

Date: 2026-11-06. Sources for `channel/episodes/colombia-2.js`. Lesson goal from the program: explain how altitude changes climate and crops. No Colombian person has reviewed this yet. **Ask one before any public release.**

Pages were read through a summarizing tool, or seen only as search extracts, so wording isn't checked word by word against each page. Check the key ones by hand before any public release.

## Why air gets colder as you climb
| What the lesson says | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|
| The Sun is about 150 million km away | [NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/sun/facts/) | |
| Being closer to the Sun isn't the reason; lots of people think it should make mountains hotter | [UT Austin physics course](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/sm1/Thermalhtml/node60.html) | |
| One small step toward a campfire on the far side of the world | **Our own sum** | 6 km out of 150 million km is 1 part in 25 million. A step of 0.8 m × 25 million = 20,000 km, about halfway around the Earth |
| Sunlight passes through the air and warms the ground, and the ground warms the air | [GLOBE lesson](https://www.globe.gov/documents/21785181/0/Heating+Things+Up.pdf/5d754e9a-1ad0-4109-b6d3-51526b2f0bfb), [Windy textbook](https://windy.app/es/textbook/atmosphere-heat-up-from-the-ground.html) | Simplified: some sunlight is absorbed by the air |
| Air is thinner high up, and it cools as it spreads out | [NOAA](https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/atmosphere/layers-of-atmosphere), [UCAR](https://scied.ucar.edu/learning-zone/atmosphere/troposphere), [UT Austin](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/sm1/Thermalhtml/node60.html) | |
| Squeezing air warms it: a bike pump gets warm | Standard physics | **No page found that uses the pump example.** Part of a pump's warmth also comes from rubbing. A fact-checker should look at this line |
| About 6 degrees colder for every 1,000 m | Average for the lower air is about 6.5 °C per 1,000 m ([HowStuffWorks summary](https://science.howstuffworks.com/nature/climate-weather/atmospheric/question186.htm), [Weather Gamut](https://www.weathergamut.com/2014/08/21)). **Our own check with real towns:** Girardot to Bogotá is 14 °C over 2,350 m, which gives 6.0 °C per 1,000 m. Girardot to Fusagasugá gives 5.6 | No IDEAM page found that states "6". It's a rule of thumb, and the lesson says "about" |

Other details:
- Medellín: 22 °C, about 1,400 to 1,500 m, called the City of Eternal Spring: [Spanish Wikipedia](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medell%C3%ADn).
- Coffee: Colombia's coffee zones are 1,000 to 2,000 m, best at 1,250 to 1,600 m, per Cenicafé as [cited by GCR](https://www.gcrmag.com/the-influence-of-latitude-on-coffee-quality). Arabica prefers 18 to 23 °C, same source. Secondary source.
- Potatoes began in the Andes: [International Potato Center](https://cipotato.org/potato/). Most of Colombia's potatoes come from Cundinamarca, Boyacá and Nariño: [Fedepapa bulletin](https://fedepapa.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Boleti%CC%81n-Nacional.pdf).
- Roses on the high plain around Bogotá, about 2,600 m, in greenhouses: [BBVA](https://www.bbva.com/es/co/economia-y-finanzas/colombia-consolida-su-liderazgo-global-con-record-historico-en-exportacion-de-flores/), [Blu Radio](https://www.bluradio.com/nacion/el-largo-camino-de-las-rosas-colombianas-hasta-san-valentin).

## The road from Girardot to Bogotá
- Girardot: town about 290 m, 28 °C, 134 km from Bogotá: [Spanish Wikipedia](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girardot_(Cundinamarca)).
- Fusagasugá: about 1,726 m, 20 °C, 59 km from Bogotá: [Spanish Wikipedia](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusagasug%C3%A1).
- Time: Rome2Rio says about 2 h 15 without traffic, Semana about 3.5 hours by one route, and more than 4 at holiday weekends: [Rome2Rio](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Bogot%C3%A1/Giradot), [Semana](https://www.semana.com/turismo/articulo/rutas-para-salir-de-bogota-a-girardot-facilmente-cuantos-peajes-tiene-cada-una/202446/), [Valora Analitik](https://www.valoraanalitik.com/ciudadanos-viajan-girardor-llegan-menos-una-hora/). The lesson says "about three hours".
- Bogotá people go to Girardot to swim: [Publimetro](https://www.publimetro.co/bogota/2024/12/03/satena-lanzo-vuelo-entre-bogota-y-girardot-para-ir-a-piscinear-sin-trancones/).
- **The family in the lesson is imagined,** and the lesson says "imagine". The road line in the drawing joins three real heights. It isn't a survey of the real road, which goes up and down in between.

## Clothes and food
- Ruana: thick sheep's wool, square, worn by farmers in the cold high plains of Cundinamarca and Boyacá: [Voice of America](https://www.vozdeamerica.com/a/la-ruana-simbolo-campesino-colombiano-que-ha-vestido-grandes-personalidades/7121755.html), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruana).
- Sombrero vueltiao: woven by Zenú people from caña flecha, Córdoba and Sucre, a national symbol: [El Universal](https://www.eluniversal.com.co/cultural/la-historia-alrededor-del-sombrero-vueltiao-XG6293426). The law number is cited differently by different pages, so the lesson doesn't give it. **No source says it was designed for the sun,** so the lesson only calls it a hat from the hot lowlands.
- Ajiaco: Bogotá's soup, chicken with three kinds of potato: [Bogotá city site](https://bogota.gov.co/mi-ciudad/desarrollo-economico/ajiaco-puchero-y-chocolate-santafereno-los-platos-tipicos-bogotanos), [Tasting Table](https://tastingtable.com/1242976/ajiaco-colombias-hearty-chicken-soup-packed-with-vegetables).

## Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta
- About 42 km from the coast, with every thermal floor from hot to permanent snow: [Colombia Travel, the official tourism site](https://www.colombia.travel/es/santa-marta/la-sierra-nevada-de-santa-marta), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada_de_Santa_Marta). Another page says 46 km.
- **Height:** older figure 5,775 m, recent measurements nearer 5,700 m ([preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.14978)). The lesson says "over 5,700 m", as Lesson 1's notes ask.
- **"Highest coastal range in the world" is too strong.** The tourism site says it, but Wikipedia says Canada's Saint Elias Mountains are higher. The lesson says "the highest coastal range in the tropics".
- A marathon is about 42 km (common knowledge).
- Cloud forest above about 1,000 m, páramo roughly 3,300 to 5,000 m, snow above: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada_de_Santa_Marta), with no specific source given there for the zones.
- The slope in our drawing is simplified. Only the sea, the peak height and the distance are real.
- Kogi, Arhuaco, Kankuamo and Wiwa peoples, about 70,000 people; their knowledge is on UNESCO's Intangible Heritage list since 2022: [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/news/2548). Wikipedia cites 30,000 for the national park alone.
- In Colombia's Caribbean region: it's in the Caribbean departments, and it's listed under that region in [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_region_of_Colombia). Some maps treat it separately.

## Páramo and frailejones
- About half of the world's páramos are in Colombia: [Caracol](https://www.noticiascaracol.com/colombia/colombia-tiene-casi-el-50-de-los-paramos-que-hay-en-el-mundo-hacen-llamado-para-protegerlos), [Semana](https://www.semana.com/sostenibilidad/articulo/los-paramos-colombianos-suministran-agua-a-17-millones-de-personas/202107/). Estimates run from 49 to 52%.
- Frailejón means "big monk": [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espeletia). Saying they look like lines of monks is our wording.
- Woolly leaves protect from cold, and dead leaves stay on the stem and keep it warmer: [El Tiempo, quoting Wildlife Conservation Society](https://www.eltiempo.com/vida/medio-ambiente/frailejon-que-son-y-por-que-su-moda-actual-658948), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Espeletia).
- Growth: 1 to 2.5 cm a year, and up to 100 years to reach 1 m: [Agronegocios](https://www.agronegocios.co/agricultura/lo-que-tal-vez-no-sabias-del-frailejon-ernesto-perez-hay-unas-81-especies-en-el-pais-3336328), [El Colombiano](https://www.elcolombiano.com/tendencias/que-son-los-frailejones-y-por-que-estan-de-moda-AN16930189). **Growth varies a lot** between plants and heights, per a [Venezuelan study](https://sciencetrends.com/growth-rates-of-andean-stem-rosettes/), so the lesson says "often" and "can".
- Leaves catch moisture and drops wet the soil; deep soils soak it up and release it slowly: [El Tiempo](https://www.eltiempo.com/vida/medio-ambiente/frailejon-que-son-y-por-que-su-moda-actual-658948), [Semana](https://www.semana.com/sostenibilidad/articulo/los-paramos-colombianos-suministran-agua-a-17-millones-de-personas/202107/).
- About 17 million Colombians drink water that starts in a páramo: [Semana, 2021](https://www.semana.com/sostenibilidad/articulo/los-paramos-colombianos-suministran-agua-a-17-millones-de-personas/202107/). That's about 1 in 3 of 53 million (our sum), and [The Nature Conservancy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fPrThd5eEI0) also says one-third.
- Chingaza: "most of Bogotá's water". Reports say about [70%](https://www.larepublica.co/economia/racionamiento-de-agua-en-bogota-busca-evitar-desabastecimiento-3834967) or 80%, and one 2025 report says almost half. **No direct page from Bogotá's water company was found.** Worth checking.
- A national law protects páramos: [Law 1930 of 2018](https://www.anla.gov.co/wanla/eureka/normativa/leyes/ley-1930-de-2018-gestion-integral-de-los-paramos-en-colombia). One [study](https://expeditiorepositorio.utadeo.edu.co/bitstreams/262f8d30-68bd-4846-9f83-bbea5db9bc86/download) finds it's only partly carried out.

## Shrinking ice
- Ice area: 347.9 km² in the 1800s, 30.83 km² in 2024, a loss of 91%: [Portafolio on IDEAM's report](https://www.portafolio.co/sostenibilidad/confirman-extincion-del-glaciar-en-el-cocuy-y-se-encienden-alertas-por-el-avance-del-cambio-climatico-491836), [Radio Nacional](https://radionacional.co/actualidad/medio-ambiente/fenomeno-de-el-nino-aceleraria-derretimiento-de-glaciares-en-colombia). IDEAM's own PDF was too big for the reading tool.
- Square about 5.5 km across: **our own sum** (√30.83 = 5.55). "About an hour and a quarter's walk" uses 4.5 km an hour.
- The Cerros de la Plaza glacier in El Cocuy reached 0 km², confirmed in March 2026: [Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/sostenibilidad/confirman-extincion-del-glaciar-en-el-cocuy-y-se-encienden-alertas-por-el-avance-del-cambio-climatico-491836).
- We left out the number of mountains with ice left, and Santa Isabel: one news site says it's already gone while IDEAM-based reports give it a few years.

- Water boils at about 92 °C at 2,400 m, and food takes longer to cook: [Wikipedia, citing NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-altitude_cooking). "A little over 90 degrees in Bogotá" is our reading of that table.
- Egan Bernal: raised in Zipaquirá at about 2,600 m, first Colombian winner of the Tour de France, 2019, aged 22: [MercoPress](https://en.mercopress.com/2019/07/29/bernal-22-becomes-the-first-colombian-to-win-the-tour-de-france), [AFP via Hürriyet](https://hurriyetdailynews.com/youthful-bernal-lifts-colombian-cycling-to-new-heights-145333). ## Not verified, or weak
1. The bike pump example (see above).
2. The "about 6 degrees" rule rests on general science plus our own sum from three towns.
3. Bogotá's monthly numbers come from Wikipedia's table without a named source for that row.
4. The share of Bogotá's water from Chingaza.
5. The market line ("trucks carry food up and down, so bananas from the hot land sit beside vegetables from the cold land") is **our inference**. The photo shows a Bogotá market with green bananas and vegetables, and guides describe fruit from many regions, for example [Publimetro](https://www.publimetro.co/gourmetro/2023/11/17/paloquemao-frutas-turistas-y-tacos/). No page says it about thermal floors.
6. IDEAM's report itself was not read, only news reports of it.
7. The Nevado del Ruiz map is a Wikimedia contributor's "own work" and names no data source. Its overall shrinkage fits IDEAM's reports, but the exact lines are unchecked. Its legend is in Spanish.
8. "Apart from the Andes": common description of the Sierra, from [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Nevada_de_Santa_Marta) and tourism pages. Not checked against a geology source.
9. "Scientists link this to Earth's warming climate": from the [Portafolio report](https://www.portafolio.co/sostenibilidad/confirman-extincion-del-glaciar-en-el-cocuy-y-se-encienden-alertas-por-el-avance-del-cambio-climatico-491836), which also names El Niño as a speeding-up factor.
10. "About 1.4 meters in about 11 years" is a typical child's height, from general knowledge. It isn't sourced, and your own children may differ.
11. Where the picture is from: the frailejones on the cover are on Nevado del Tolima, and the páramo photos are from other places than the Sierra. The Sierra scene doesn't use them. Picture titles and pages on Wikimedia Commons are the only evidence of each place.
