## Safety decisions
- **Nobody has to share a real feeling.** Shout-it-out questions use stories. The "try it" is private.
- **The test checks knowledge only,** and nothing about how a child feels is asked or saved.
- **A magic wand, not a pill, in the hook.** The fact-checker pointed out that a pill could shame children on real medicine.
- **Telling someone:** includes adults outside the home, in case home is the problem, and a line on hurting yourself.
- **Owner to decide:** which real helpline to name. It depends on the country, so the lesson only says "many countries have a phone line just for children". I couldn't load Childline or NSPCC pages to check them.

## Unverified: my judgment, not from a source
- **Nervousness: "this matters".** Folk wisdom.
- **Surprise: "stop and look".** Found in no source I could load. Worded as "can".
- **The sadness line "when people see you're sad, they may come and help"** is close to Noba's distress and aid, not exact.
- **"Tell someone when..." list:** my reading of the guidance's "triggers for seeking support". It isn't a quote. A child-safeguarding professional hasn't reviewed it, and **should before any paid launch.**
- **The Lieberman 2007 study, the "90 seconds" claim, and "30 to 50%" figures** are left out, because I couldn't verify them.
