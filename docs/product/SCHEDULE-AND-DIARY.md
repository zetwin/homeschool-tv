# Timetable, diary and program document

Last updated: 2026-11-06. The owner's idea, written out: a normal school week, with different subjects each day, and a personal diary for each child, like the Ukrainian "щоденник". Lessons follow the timetable. We use norms from real schools and invent nothing.

Sources and what is unverified: `docs/research/2026-11-06-school-norms.md`.

## The idea in one picture
Program (what to learn, in what order) + Timetable (when) = **dated classes**, which fill each child's **diary**, with homework and marks.

## Three pages
| Page | Who, where | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Program document** | Anyone, on a laptop. Can be printed | The whole year on one page, like a formal PDF. Each unit is a link |
| **Diary** | One child on their device | This week: classes, homework, marks, a parent's signature |
| **The week** | A parent on the iPad or phone | The same week for one child: what was done, the note and the signature. See "The week, for parents" |
| **Timetable** | A parent on the iPad, laptop or phone | Set the week, the school year, breaks, and how test scores show |

The **TV home** shows today's classes. **My day** stays as the child's short to-do list.

## Norms we follow
| Question | Real schools | What we use |
|---|---|---|
| Lessons per week | England: about 25 one-hour lessons. Ukraine 7th grade: 32 | **25** by default. The parent can change it |
| Weeks in a year | England: 190 days, about 39 weeks | **39** |
| Subject share | Published Year 7 timetables: English 4, Maths 4, Science 3, humanities and languages about 2 | Calculated from the program below. Matches closely |
| Class length | England about 50 to 60 minutes, Ukraine usually 45 | **45**, changeable |
| Marks | Ukraine 1 to 12 | **Out of 12**, or percent |
| Home education | England: no timetable, set hours or school terms needed | So all of this is the parent's choice, and every setting can change |

Rules used: a subject appears once a day at most, core subjects come first, and its classes are spread across the week. Friday is lighter.

## How lessons follow the timetable
1. Each subject has a list: its lessons in order, with an exam at the end of each unit.
2. Starting on the first school day, each class takes the next item in that subject's list.
3. Breaks and days off are skipped, so classes **move to the next school day** and nothing is lost.
4. Change the timetable, and the dates recalculate.

Homework and marks come from what really happens: tests and exams the child took.

## The diary
Same layout as a paper щоденник. A two-page spread: Monday to Wednesday on the left, Thursday, Friday and "This week" on the right.

Each class has: number, subject, topic, homework, mark.
- **Tap a class** to open it: watch the lesson, take the test, see the goal.
- **Homework:** before the lesson is watched, a quiet "Watch today" or "Catch up: watch it". After it's watched, the lesson test, by the next class in that subject, counted from the class day or the day it was watched, whichever is later. Then re-tests at 1, 7 and 30 days. A parent can also write any homework.
- **Red** means really late: the lesson was watched, and the test's date has passed. Nothing else in the homework column is red.
- **A class with no lesson yet** stays grey, with no repeated text. "This week" says how many there are, and tapping the class explains.
- **Marks:** from tests and exams, or written by a parent.
- **This week:** classes done, tests to do, exams, classes without a lesson yet, the parent's note and **signature**.
- **Text size on a child's tablet:** 15 and 13.5 pixels at 1024×768, 16 and 14 at 1180×820. Measured as the largest that still fits a whole week on one screen.
- **Phone:** week strip on top, one day at a time. If a day is long, it scrolls inside the diary, above the tabs.

## The week, for parents (Board style, 2026-11-10)
The parent's week is drawn in the parent pad's Board style, not as the paper щоденник. The owner said yes to this plan on 2026-11-10.
- **Top:** child buttons with faces; ‹ week › and "This week" when another week is shown.
- **The child's card,** in the child's colour: face, "Kid 1's week", a progress circle, then plain words: "0 of 25 classes done. 3 exams: English on Monday, Science on Wednesday and History on Thursday." (tests to do only when there are some), the parent's note and **Sign this week** (then "✓ Signed 10 Nov").
- **The days:** five cards side by side on a landscape iPad or TV. Each class shows time, subject, the whole topic (never cut with "…"), an Exam tag, a green tick when done, "Not done" on past days, and a small play mark when a lesson is ready. "No lesson yet" isn't repeated on each row; tapping such a class says so. Today's card has a dark outline and a Today tag. A quiet **No school this day** link sits at the bottom of today and later days; a day off says why and offers **Back to school**.
- **One day at a time** on a portrait iPad, a phone, and with bigger text on screens under 1500 px wide: a row of day buttons (Mon 9 … Fri 13, with an Exam or Off tag), then that day. On a phone the note and Sign this week come after the day.
- **Tap a class:** a card with the child, the day and time, subject, topic, where it sits in the unit, the goal, whether the child has done it, and **Play the lesson** when it exists. Parents don't give marks, set homework or rate lessons here any more (owner, 2026-11-09).

## The timetable (Board style, 2026-11-10)
- **Top:** Everyone or one child, with faces, and **School year and breaks**.
- **The grid:** five days by six classes, each a block tinted in the subject's colour with a colour bar; free classes are dashed. Tapping a block opens a card of big subject buttons, the current one ticked. A sentence says who a change is for: everyone (except children with their own), or that a change gives this child their own timetable.
- **Enough classes?** beside the grid (under it on a portrait iPad): a one-sentence answer, then only the subjects that need more, with a button **Put Emotional Intelligence in a free class** (it opens the first free class), and one line for the rest: "7 other subjects have enough classes." Activities like PE and Art have no program, so they aren't counted. The subject picker also shows "Needs 1 more".
- **School year and breaks** opens as a card: first day, when the day starts, class length, how test scores show (out of 12 or percent; it was called "Marks"), the breaks with Remove, and Add a break. A last day before the first day is refused with a message.
- **Phone:** day buttons, then that day's six classes, then Enough classes?.

## The program document
- First page: **terms across subjects,** the way schools publish a curriculum map. Fits one screen on a laptop.
- Then how the year is organized, then one numbered section per subject with every unit and known lesson.
- Marks where you are and what's ready. Prints as a PDF, with links to Oak's programs.
- Claims only that it "follows". Not approved or accredited.

## Did the owner miss anything? Suggestions
1. **Term report** (табель): each subject's marks and a comment. Easy once marks exist. **Recommended next.**
2. **Attendance and hours:** some countries ask home-educating families for records. Check the rules for your own country.
3. **Year 6 and Year 8 programs:** one child is in Year 6, and only Year 7 is loaded for all subjects.
4. **Reminders** for parents, by email. No child email or notifications.
5. **Spanish-language screens,** as planned.
6. **Which country's rules?** Ukraine, Colombia or England changes dates, subjects and records.
7. **Real-world subjects:** PE, Art, Music, and Religious Education or Citizenship if your family wants them.
8. **Breaks in the day:** we schedule them, but the app doesn't nudge kids away from screens yet.
