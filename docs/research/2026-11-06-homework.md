# Homework research

Date: 2026-11-06. From search extracts and summaries, not the full studies. Check the originals before quoting these. Nothing here covers children's own preferences, and no one has tested our design with children.

## What the evidence says
| Finding | Source | How sure |
|---|---|---|
| Homework links to better results much more strongly from grades 7 to 12 than in primary school | [Cooper 2006, Duke summary](https://today.duke.edu/2006/03/homework.html) | A review of 60+ studies, but it shows a link, not proof of cause |
| Below about grade 5, the link is close to zero | [Cooper, via WHYY](https://whyy.org/articles/how-much-time-should-your-child-spend-on-homework-each-night) | Second-hand |
| Too much stops helping. Above about 2 hours, older students gained no more | [Duke summary](https://today.duke.edu/2006/03/homework.html) | Upper high school only |
| Rule of thumb: 10 minutes a night per grade. Grade 6 is about 60, grade 7 about 70 | [Cooper, via WHYY](https://whyy.org/articles/how-much-time-should-your-child-spend-on-homework-each-night) | A common habit, and sources disagree on how far research supports it |
| Secondary homework is linked to about 5 months' extra progress | [EEF, via Structural Learning](https://structural-learning.com/post/homework-evidence-based-policy-guide) | Moderate evidence. I couldn't reach the EEF's own homework page |
| Practice and review tasks work better than new learning or long projects without help | [Structural Learning](https://structural-learning.com/post/homework-evidence-based-policy-guide) | Second-hand |
| 15 to 30 minutes with a clear aim is often called best | [Structural Learning](https://structural-learning.com/post/homework-evidence-based-policy-guide), citing Cooper | Second-hand, check it |
| Feedback is among the strongest things schools do | [EEF toolkit, via Structural Learning](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/eef-teaching-learning-toolkit-guide) | Second-hand |

## What this changes in our plan
1. **The limits are ceilings, not targets.** Ukraine allows 1 to 1.5 hours, but research favors short, focused work, and our children also take a test after each lesson.
2. **Starting amount stays low, then can rise:** 20 to 30 minutes for ages 10 to 12, with the parent able to raise it. Above about 60 to 70 minutes, the app warns.
3. **Practice comes first.** Re-tests and fixing mistakes are our strongest kind of homework.
4. **Projects need a check-in.** Long projects with no help did worst, so a project gets steps and a parent check.
5. **Every piece gets feedback,** automatic or a parent's comment. Homework with no feedback is the weakest.
