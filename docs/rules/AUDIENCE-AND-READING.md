# Who reads this, and how

Last updated: 2026-11-06. **Read this before any screen is designed.** The owner rejected all of design round one because it wasn't designed around its readers.

This is my current understanding, and the owner should correct it. Distances and timings are my estimates, except that Apple cites [8 feet or more](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-tvos) for TV.

## Three readers, three situations
| | Children together | One child | Parent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Device** | TV | Tablet, laptop or phone | Laptop or phone |
| **Distance** | Sofa, 2–4 m | 30–50 cm | 30–60 cm |
| **Input** | Remote | Touch or keyboard | Mouse or touch |
| **How they read** | A glance. Pictures first, a few words | Normal reading, one task at a time | Quick scan for facts |
| **Their question** | "What are we watching? Is it cool?" | "What do I do now? How long? How did I do?" | "Are they on track? What needs me?" |
| **Time on screen** | Seconds, then press play | Minutes per task | About a minute, some days longer |
| **Text** | One short line, large | Normal sizes, full sentences | **Large: body 20 px at least on the iPad.** Some parents can't read small details. Full width, never a narrow column |
| **Mood** | Excited | Focused | Calm, wants trust |

## What goes where
| Content | Main place | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Choosing and watching a lesson | TV | Shared and exciting |
| My day, tests, exams, results | Child's device | Personal, needs reading and answering |
| Status, gradebook, marking, billing | Parent's laptop or phone | Detail and decisions |
| The whole year | Parent's screen first; a child's device for curious children | It's long text. It can't be read from a sofa |
| One subject or term on TV | TV, only if a child wants it | A few large items at a time |

## Reading order comes before style
For each screen, write this down before any drawing:
1. Who is reading, on which device, from how far?
2. What's the one thing they want to know?
3. What do they read first, second and third?
4. What do they do next?
5. What can be removed?

## Rules from this
- **The biggest thing on a screen is the most useful thing.** Not the prettiest.
- **Use real words.** No codes, no sideways text.
- **Speak the reader's language.** Children don't think in "units" or "lesson 1 of 4". Parents do.
- **Size follows distance.** Huge text on a tablet or laptop is as wrong as tiny text on a TV.
- **Show an actual size test.** Check TV screens from the sofa, not on a laptop.
- **The design must work for every subject,** not only the most photogenic one. Test each design with Fractions, The Tempest and Magna Carta.
- **Respect children's age.** Not babyish, not corporate, and not scary.
- **Parents' screens can be dense.** A good table is a kindness there, not a failure.

## What was wrong in round one
- **It contradicted our own flow.** Tests are meant to happen on each child's device, yet I drew them as TV posters. "My day" was also drawn for TV.
- **One format for everyone.** Every screen was a 1920×1080 poster, whoever reads it.
- **"Friday" was the biggest word on Today,** the least useful thing there.
- **The Year chart used codes such as "Fm" and "Ar".** They mean nothing unless learned.
- **The metro map used tilted text.** It's tiring to read, especially from a distance.
- **"Your year" showed 71 topics in small text on TV.** That's a parent's document, drawn as a child's TV screen.
- **The skeleton was on nearly every screen.** A staring skull and a glowing X-ray may feel creepy or like a horror poster. I chose them for impact.
- **Only bones were designed.** I never tried it with fractions or Shakespeare, so I don't know if it works as a system.
- **School words everywhere:** starter quiz, unit, lesson test.
- **I didn't test with a child.** I asked which style looked best, and that's the wrong first question.

## How to test
- **Five-second test on a child:** show a screen for five seconds, hide it, then ask "What was it? What would you press?"
- **Sofa test:** can it be read on the real TV from the real sofa?
- **Parent test:** can the parent tell who needs help, within 30 seconds?
- **Ask children what they already love.** The YouTube channels, games and books they choose are better guides than my taste.
