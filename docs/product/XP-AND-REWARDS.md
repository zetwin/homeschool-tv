# XP and rewards

**Status: a plan for the owner to read and answer. Nothing here is built.** Written 2026-11-10. The children's wish, in the owner's words, is in the team's private notes (2026-11-10). Research, with links: `research/2026-11-10-xp-and-rewards.md`.

## The idea in one paragraph
Children earn **XP** for the work they do: lessons, tests, fixing mistakes, exams, homework, habits like chess and reading, and household tasks. XP is for **doing and getting better, never for marks**, so a 10-year-old earns as much as a 12-year-old for the same work. Children **spend XP in a family shop**. The parents make the shop: a movie night, a sweet, a later bedtime, choosing dinner, a trip. A child buys, a parent hands it over. Children can **save up** for something big, and add their own **wishes**. There's also a **family goal** all three children fill together, so they help each other instead of competing. XP stays inside the family: no ranking, no ads, and no money ever buys XP.

## Why it's shaped this way
The children asked for this, which is a good start: choosing for yourself keeps motivation alive. But research gives four warnings, and the plan answers each.

| The evidence says | So we |
|---|---|
| **Promised prizes can make children like a task less,** more so for children than adults. Surprise rewards don't do this. Dull tasks aren't harmed. | Keep XP modest for things they may already enjoy (activities, reading, chess). Give more XP for dull jobs, like chores. Make parents' surprise bonuses easy |
| **Rewarding scores works worse than rewarding actions.** Children don't know how to "get a better score", but they do know how to "read the book" | XP for finishing, fixing mistakes and improving. **Never for the mark** |
| **Leaderboards and badges** lowered motivation and results in a classroom study | No ranking between siblings. A **family goal** instead |
| **Children find the fastest way to points** ("gaming the system") | XP once per thing. Nothing for re-watching, retaking for points, or rushing |

It also fits rules we already have: "No prizes promised for marks" and "no streak that resets to zero" (`ACTIVITIES.md`), and "No loot boxes, random prizes, paying, or guilt about streaks" (`rules/TEST-AND-EXAM-RULES.md`).

**The honest part:** XP for learning is a promised reward, which is the risky kind. We keep it because the children asked, because it's modest, and because the family will watch for warning signs (see "Watch for these signs").

## What earns XP
A normal school day comes to **about 100 XP**, and a school week to **about 500**. Every amount below can be changed by a parent.

### Learning
| Thing | XP | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| **Lesson watched on the child's own device**, to the end | 15 | Once per lesson. Only if most of it really played (about 80%). Re-watching gives nothing |
| **Lesson watched on the TV** | 15 to the **family goal** | The TV doesn't know who's in the room. Each child's own XP then comes from the test on their own device |
| **Starter quiz** | 5 | Once per lesson |
| **Lesson test, finished** | 20 | Once per test. **The same for any score.** A rushed test gives nothing (see below) |
| **Fix my mistakes, all fixed** | 10 | Once per test. Correcting mistakes is the most useful thing a child can do after a test |
| **Second try beats the first** | +10 | Once per test. Rewards getting better, not being best |
| **Re-test** (after 1, 7 and 30 days) | 10 each | Once each |
| **Unit exam, finished,** including the child's own check of the written answers | 60 | **The same for any score.** So there's no reason to tick extra points when self-checking |
| **Homework** (real-world task, read, write, prepare) | 10 to 20 | Kinds a parent checks need the parent's OK first (`HOMEWORK.md`) |
| **Project** | 50 | Parent's OK |
| **Materials activity** (crossword, secret code and so on) | 5 | At most 4 a day count. The **stamp** stays the main reward there (`ACTIVITIES.md`) |

### Habits and home
| Thing | XP | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| **Habit done today** (chess, read a hard-cover book, and any the parents add) | 10 each | Once a day per habit. The child ticks it; a parent can undo it with a note |
| **Household task** the parents add (tidy the room, help cook, wash the car) | Parent sets, 5 to 50 | **A parent's OK before the XP arrives**, as in Joon and OurHome. A task can be one-off or weekly |
| **Parent's bonus** | 10, 25 or 50 | Always with a short note saying what was good: "You explained fractions to your brother." Either parent can give it |

### Weekly
| Thing | XP | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| **A good school week:** something done on 4 of 5 school days | +25 | See "Kind streaks" |

### Example: one school day
Lesson on the TV (family goal) + starter quiz 5 + test 20 + fixed mistakes 10 + a re-test 10 + one homework 15 + chess 10 + reading 10 + set the table 10 = **90 XP**, plus anything a parent adds.

## Stopping the shortcuts
| Shortcut | What stops it |
|---|---|
| Re-watching a lesson | XP once per lesson per child |
| Skipping through a lesson | XP only if most of it really played. The player already knows where you jumped |
| Retaking a test for points | Finishing XP once. A retake can earn only "Second try beats the first", once |
| Tapping answers fast to finish | If answers come too fast (for example, most in under 3 seconds) and the score is low, no XP, and a friendly message: "That was very fast. Want to try again slowly?" Never "cheater" |
| Ticking extra points in an exam self-check | Exam XP doesn't depend on the score |
| Ticking a habit that wasn't done | Parents see habit ticks and can undo one with a note. Trust first |
| Ticking a chore that wasn't done | No XP until a parent approves |
| Lots of easy activities | At most 4 activities a day earn XP |
| Asking a parent for bonuses | Bonuses are the parent's choice. Children can't request them |

## Levels
- **Your level counts every XP you've ever earned. It never goes down,** even when you spend. Spending uses "XP to spend", which is a second number. Habitica and Duolingo also keep the two apart.
- The child sees: **Level 7, 340 XP to spend.**
- Each level needs a little more XP than the last. The first levels come in days, later ones in a few weeks.
- A new level is a small celebration, and parents see it on the pad. It **unlocks nothing to buy**, so it stays a sign of effort, not a shop.

## Kind streaks
- **A week, not a chain of days.** The child sees "4 of 5 school days this week", never "Day 37".
- **Weekends, holidays and sick days never count against anyone.** When a parent marks "No school today", that day is a rest day by itself.
- **Nothing ever drops to zero.** A missed day just leaves a gap in this week.
- **Rest days are free and automatic.** They're never bought with XP. Duolingo sells streak repairs; that's called a deceptive pattern.
- Habits get the same idea: "Chess 5 times this week".

## Badges
**We don't add a new badge system.** `ACTIVITIES.md` already gives every subject a **passport with stamps**, and that stays the badge. We add a few **milestone stamps** for things outside activities:
- "Fixed 50 mistakes"
- "30 chess days", "10 books read"
- "Finished the unit: The human body"

Each one says what was learned or practised, not how many points. No badge for a high mark.

## The family goal
- **One weekly target for the whole family.** Every child's XP adds to it, and so do lessons watched on the TV.
- **One reward everyone shares,** chosen by the parents (or voted on by the children): "Pizza and a film on Saturday".
- The target is set for the number of children, so it works for one child or five. Suggested: about 80% of what the children usually earn together, so it's reachable but needs everyone.
- **Helping a sibling counts.** Parents can give a bonus "for helping", which also fills the family goal.
- The TV shows the family goal at the end of a lesson. **Never one child's XP on the TV.**

## Fair for different ages
- **XP for doing, not for scores.** Finishing a test is the same work at 10 and at 12.
- **The same prices for every child.**
- **Same rules for all, written where children can read them:** a "How XP works" card on the child's XP page. Children accept differences when they understand the reason (research: fairness matters more than sameness).
- If one child has much more work in a week (a different year group), parents see the weekly totals side by side on the pad and can add a bonus. Children never see each other's totals in the app.

## The reward shop

### What parents can put in it
Each reward has: a **name**, a **picture** (from a set of icons), a **price**, a **limit** (for example "once a week"), **when** it can be used (for example "weekends only"), and **who** it's for (all children, or one).

A suggested starting price list, for about 500 XP a week:

| Size | Price | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Small**, now and then | 40 to 80 | A sweet or a small treat. Choose the music in the car. 15 minutes more play |
| **Medium** | 150 to 300 | Choose tonight's dinner. Bedtime 30 minutes later (weekends). 30 minutes of a game. A board game with a parent. Skip one household task |
| **Big**, about a week or two | 500 to 900 | Family movie night where you choose the film. A cinema trip. A friend sleeps over |
| **Saving up**, a month or more | 1,500 to 3,000 | A day trip you choose. A new book or a building set. A pocket-money amount |

- **Sweets and screen time are allowed: parents decide.** The research is weak for both, but it leans one way: screens used as rewards go with more screen time. So the starting list leads with other rewards, and parents can set a **weekly limit** on screen-time rewards, such as "at most 1 hour a week from the shop".
- **Pocket money** can be a reward, but the app never touches money. It only records "£2, given by Mum". There's no exchange rate, no cards and no payments inside the app.
- **Price check:** if a child is saving huge amounts, rewards are too dear or too few. If they spend everything at once, they're too cheap. The pad can say so in one line.

### How a child buys, and a parent hands it over
1. The child opens the shop and taps a reward. They see the price and their XP to spend. One tap: **"I want this"**.
2. The XP is **set aside**, not lost yet. The reward shows as **Waiting for Mum or Dad**.
3. On the pad, either parent sees: **"[Child] wants Movie night, 600 XP"** with three buttons:
   - **Give it now**: done. The XP is spent.
   - **Plan it**: pick a day ("Saturday"). The child sees "Planned for Saturday".
   - **Not now**: with a short reason. **All the XP goes back.**
4. When a planned reward has happened, the parent taps **Given**. Or it closes itself on its day, if the parent prefers.
5. The child's history shows every purchase and who handed it over.

Small rewards can be set to **"no OK needed"**, so they're done in one tap and the parent just sees them in the list. Big ones always need an OK.

If a request waits more than 3 days, the pad reminds the parent once, quietly. No alarms.

### Saving up
- A child can **pin one reward as their goal**. A progress ring shows how close it is: "Day trip: 1,240 of 2,000".
- The goal can change any time. Nothing is lost.
- XP doesn't run out. Saving is a skill too.

### The children's wish list
- A child can **add a wish** in a few words, with a picture: "Go to the climbing wall".
- Only parents see it. On the pad: **Add to the shop** (choose a price) or **Not now** (with a kind note).
- This is how the children help build the shop, which the research says matters: choice keeps motivation alive. Recommended: at the start, sit down together and fill the shop with them.

## The parent's side
The pad is for "doing and leaving" (`PARENT-PAD-PURPOSE.md`). XP must add **almost no work**.

| What | Where | Taps |
|---|---|---|
| **Requests and tasks to OK** | One line on Today, **only when something waits**: "2 rewards and 1 task to check" | 1 to open, 1 per item |
| **Give a bonus** | From a child on Today, or the Rewards page: child, 10/25/50, a note (typed or spoken) | 3 or 4 |
| **The shop** | The Rewards page: add, change, hide, set limits | Rare |
| **Each child's XP** | The Rewards page: level, XP to spend, saving goal, this week's total, history | One tap |
| **Household tasks and habits** | The Rewards page: add a task with its XP; weekly or one-off | Rare |
| **Settings** | Admin, Learning plan: XP amounts, daily limits, **turn XP off** for everything or one kind | Very rare |

- **Two parents:** either can approve, give or refuse. The history shows who did it.
- **Large text** on every part, for parents who need it (`rules/VISUAL-HIERARCHY.md`).
- **Where the Rewards page lives** is an open question: the bottom bar must stay the same on every page, so adding a button there is the owner's choice.
- **Undo, never punish:** a parent can undo XP given by mistake, with a note the child sees. There's no button to fine a child.

## The child's side

### My day
- A **small XP counter** at the top: "Level 7, 340 XP". It's a supporting element. The main thing on My day stays **what to do now** (the owner's note: one child said it's not clear what to do).
- After finishing something, a short message: **"+20 XP. Test finished."** or **"+10 XP. You fixed 3 mistakes."** It always says why, so the XP tells them what they did well.
- **No XP labels on To do items.** Showing prices before every task makes children pick by points. The "How XP works" card tells them everything.

### My XP page (tap the counter)
No new tab: the owner allowed only one new tab, and that's for activities and games. The counter opens one page with:
1. **Level and XP to spend**, with the saving goal's ring.
2. **The shop**: rewards with pictures and prices. Ones they can't afford yet are shown with "40 more XP".
3. **My rewards**: waiting, planned, given.
4. **My wishes**: add one.
5. **This week**: the school days ring, habits, and the family goal.
6. **History**: what earned XP, day by day.
7. **How XP works**: the rules in short sentences.

### On the TV
- **The family goal only,** at the end of a lesson: "Family goal: 1,240 of 2,000. Pizza and a film on Saturday!"
- Never one child's XP, level or purchases. Siblings in the same room shouldn't be compared on the big screen.

### What stays apart
- **Marks, the Gradebook and the Diary don't change.** XP is not a mark, and parents never see XP instead of marks.
- **Exams stay calm.** No XP messages during an exam; the XP appears after the result.
- **Tests stay tests** (`rules/TEST-AND-EXAM-RULES.md`). The XP message comes after the result, not on each question.

## Watch for these signs
Look after about 4 weeks, with the children.

| Sign | What to change |
|---|---|
| "How many XP is it?" before every task | Lower XP on learning; more surprise bonuses and the family goal |
| Refusing anything without XP | Same, and talk about it as a family |
| Rushing tests or activities | Check the fast-answer rule; lower activity XP |
| Arguments between siblings about XP | More weight on the family goal; check the rules feel fair to each child |
| Reading or chess dropping when XP is low | Make those habits "no XP, just a tick", and praise instead |
| Saving huge amounts, or spending at once | Adjust prices |

If it isn't helping, XP can be **turned off** in one place, and nothing else in the app changes.

## Risks and rules
| Risk | Rule |
|---|---|
| Rewards lower interest in learning | Modest XP for learning, more for dull jobs. Praise and surprises. Watch the signs above |
| Marks become a price | XP never depends on a score, in tests or exams |
| Sibling competition | No ranking, no totals of other children on child screens or the TV, a family goal |
| Streak stress | Weekly count, automatic rest days, nothing resets to zero, nothing sold |
| Gaming | XP once per thing, daily limits, fast-answer check, parent OK for chores |
| Too much screen time or sweets | Parents decide; the starting list leads with other rewards; weekly limits |
| More work for parents | One line on Today, only when something waits. Small rewards need no OK |

**Children's data (project rule 5):**
- XP, purchases and wishes are **family data**, stored with the rest (`data/family.json` today), and never leave the family.
- **No public leaderboards, ever.** No comparing with other families' children, not even "top 10%".
- **No ads, no brands in the shop,** no sponsored rewards, no vouchers from shops.
- **No buying XP with money. No selling rest days, levels or items.** Nothing in the app costs money except the family's own subscription.
- The activity log (`MONITORING.md`) doesn't need XP. The XP history is the family's own record, like marks.

## Later, as a paid service
- **Each family makes its own shop.** We give a **starter list** of ideas (mostly non-food, non-screen), already priced for that family. They can delete or change everything.
- **Prices adjust to the family.** After two weeks, we suggest prices from how much XP their children really earn.
- **XP is optional.** Some parents don't want rewards at all; one switch turns it off, and the app works the same without it.
- **The XP amounts are the same for every family** at the start, so it's easy to explain; parents can change them.
- **Reward ideas in each language.** Spanish-speaking families get Spanish examples.
- **Sibling price:** XP doesn't change with the plan or the number of children. Nothing in XP is a paid extra.
- **Before any other family joins:** check the law on children's data in their country, and that "rewards" don't count as anything legally like a prize or money (**not checked**).
