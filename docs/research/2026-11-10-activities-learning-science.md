# Activities: what helps children remember (2026-11-10)

Research for `product/ACTIVITIES.md`. Gathered by a research helper from the web. Items marked **UNVERIFIED** couldn't be checked on a real page or are our own judgement.

Two words used below:
- **Recall** (also called retrieval practice): bringing an answer out of memory without looking.
- **Spacing**: coming back to the same thing over several days.

## 1. What helps, and what is mostly fun
Dunlosky and others (2013) compared ten study methods. Only self-testing and spacing were rated "high value"; re-reading and highlighting were low. [APS summary](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html). Classroom quizzes improved later exam results for 6th and 8th graders. [ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ956134)

| Helps a lot | Helps some | Mostly fun |
|---|---|---|
| Quizzes, flashcards answered before flipping, crosswords whose clues need recall, "explain it" questions, mixed maths practice | Timelines and sorting from memory. Drawing a word helps recall ([Waterloo](https://uwaterloo.ca/psychology/news/need-remember-something-better-draw-it-study-finds), tested on adults) | Word search (pattern hunting, weak studies only: [example](https://publikasi.polije.ac.id/jeapco/article/view/3927)). Colour-by-answer (**UNVERIFIED**: the colouring takes longer than the thinking). Mazes, unless each turn is a question. Escape rooms: no rigorous studies found (**UNVERIFIED**) |

**How to make the fun ones useful:**
- **Word search:** give the meanings, not the words. Children remember better what they work out themselves ("generation effect", [Slamecka 1978](https://andymatuschak.org/prompts/Slamecka1978.pdf)).
- **Escape rooms and mazes:** every lock or turn is a recall question.
- **Colour-by-answer:** keep the colouring small.

## 2. Rules for a good activity
- **Recall before choices.** Benefit shown a week later. [Roediger and Karpicke 2006](https://psychology.ecu.edu/wp-content/pv-uploads/sites/216/2019/03/Roediger-Karpicke-2006.pdf)
- **Spread practice over days.** The further away the test, the wider the gaps. [Cepeda 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/)
- **A short daily review of earlier topics**, and aim for a high success rate. [Rosenshine](https://www.aft.org/sites/default/files/periodicals/Rosenshine.pdf) (link not opened)
- **Mix topics, especially in maths.** 7th graders given mixed problems scored higher, and more so later. [IES](https://ies.ed.gov/use-work/awards/interleaved-mathematics-practice)
- **Always show the right answer after a multiple-choice question**, or children can remember the wrong options. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4094137)
- **Feedback that is timely and says the next step.** EEF says the evidence is limited. [EEF](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/eef-publishes-new-guidance-report-teacher-feedback-to-improve-pupil-learning)
- **Worked examples for new maths**, with steps removed gradually. Drop them once the child is fluent. [Summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worked-example_effect) (Sweller and Cooper 1985)
- These match the [Learning Scientists' six strategies](https://www.learningscientists.org/blog/2019/11/28-1).

## 3. Game parts: what helps and what harms
- **Overall:** a small-to-medium learning gain (g = 0.49). Effects on motivation were less reliable. [Meta-analysis](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/frontdoor/index/index/docId/109056)
- **Promised prizes can lower children's own interest; praise doesn't.** [Deci 1999](https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf)
  - Children promised an award drew less afterwards; those given a surprise award didn't. [Summary](https://www.psychologynoteshq.com/overjustification-effect/)
- **Badges and a leaderboard** in one course ended with lower exam scores and lower motivation. [UCLA](https://www.smhp.psych.ucla.edu/pdfdocs/gamil.pdf)
- **For us:**
  - Stamps record what was learned, not pay.
  - Some rewards come as surprises.
  - Praise effort and strategy.
- **No ranking between siblings.** A meta-analysis reportedly favours no leaderboards (**UNVERIFIED**). No research found on sibling competition. Compare each child with their own past, or use a family goal (**UNVERIFIED**, our judgement).
- **Streaks:** the evidence is Duolingo's own, a link rather than a cause. [Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit) Use forgiving streaks, like "5 of 7 days".

## 4. Paper or device
- **Paper is better for fact texts.** It beat screens across 54 studies, more so under time pressure; for stories there was no difference. [Haifa](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/dont-throw-away-your-printed-books-a-meta-analysis-on-the-effects/)
- **Young children:** plain digital books gave lower understanding. [AERA](https://www.aera.net/Newsroom/A-Comparison-of-Childrens-Reading-on-Paper-Versus-Screen-A-Meta-Analysis)
- **Handwriting letters** engaged reading areas in 5-year-olds. [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4274624)
  - Adult studies are weaker and criticised: [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1517235/pdf).
  - "Notes by hand beat laptop" did not repeat: [APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/no-benefit-handwriting-notes.html).
- **A device is better** for instant feedback, self-quizzing and automatic spacing. It helps only when used for those. [EEF digital](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/digital)
- **Our judgement (UNVERIFIED):** paper for drawing, long writing, maths working and crosswords; the device for checking and spacing.

## 5. Kumon-style daily practice
- **Independent trial in Bangladesh:** gains in maths and self-esteem, partly lasting 20 months later. [UCL](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10177434) The very large effect mixes speed with accuracy, and that setting is very different from ours.
- **A Michigan district paper** had no comparison group. [ASEE](https://peer.asee.org/15405)
- **Kumon's own description** (marketing): about 30 minutes a day per subject, repeat until 100%. [Kumon guide](https://www.kumon.com/assets/us-en/pdfs/KUMON_PARENTS_GUIDE.pdf)
- **Where research agrees:** short daily practice, mastery, high success rates.
- **Where it disagrees:** long blocks of one problem type; mixing works better.
- **Our design:** a short daily sheet, mostly today's skill, plus a few mixed review items.

## Weak spots
Most studies are on college students or short. Nothing rigorous on escape rooms, colour-by-number or sibling competition. No studies of paper versus screen for 10 to 12-year-olds doing practice tasks.
