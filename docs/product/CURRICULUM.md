# Curriculum

Last updated: 2026-11-05

## The term
What the owner described is a **curriculum map**, or **scope and sequence**: what is taught, and in what order.

## Is there one official world standard?
No. Each country or region sets its own. For a proper program we follow one official framework, then use a teaching order built on it.

| Framework | Who sets it | Public? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **England's National Curriculum** | UK Department for Education | Yes, Open Government Licence v3.0 | Clear year-by-year science for ages 5–11, then key stages. Our recommended base |
| **Oak National Academy** | Public body sponsored by England's Department for Education | Yes, free | A complete teaching order and full lessons built for the National Curriculum |
| Next Generation Science Standards | US multi-state standards | Yes, but trademark and permission rules need checking | Used across many US states, not all |
| Cambridge Lower Secondary | Cambridge International | Full framework for registered schools only | Useful internationally; needs a relationship with Cambridge |
| Spain: LOMLOE | National minimum (Real Decreto 157/2022), with regional detail | Yes | Each region adds its own curriculum, so needs regional mapping |

**Who has "approved" it?** The National Curriculum is a government document. Oak is a government-sponsored public body whose curriculum was developed with subject partners. Our own lessons have **no** approval from anyone. We can say we follow the curriculum, but not that we're approved.

| Lesson | 1 of 4: The human skeleton |

### Official wording (England, key stage 3)
The skeletal and muscular systems cover:
- the structure and functions of the human skeleton, including support, protection, movement and making blood cells
- biomechanics: how skeleton and muscles interact, including measuring force exerted by different muscles
- the function of muscles and examples of antagonistic muscles

Other sections alongside it: cells and organisation; nutrition and digestion; gas exchange systems; reproduction; health.

### What comes after the skeleton
1. **Lesson 2: Muscles.** What muscles are made of and how they move.
2. **Lesson 3: Movement.** How muscles work together to move bones at joints.
3. **Lesson 4: Biomechanics.** Calculating the moment of a force around a joint.
4. **Unit exam.**
5. Next unit, depending on the route:
   - Oak's exact order: Heating and cooling (physics)
   - Next biology unit: Diffusion
   - Next human body unit: Human digestive system (Year 8)

### What comes before
- Year 3: skeletons and muscles, first introduction
- Year 4: digestive system and teeth
- Year 6: circulatory system; diet, exercise and lifestyle
- Year 7: Cells. Oak's Cells page says it prepares pupils for this unit, though six other units sit between them in the listed order.

## Three children, one program
Suggested: choose a shared science unit, watch together on the TV, and give each child a test at their level. Maths and English usually need separate levels per child.

The term split in map direction C is our suggestion, not an official one.
