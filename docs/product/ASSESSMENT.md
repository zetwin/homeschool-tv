# Tests and exams

Last updated: 2026-11-05

## How schools do it
Schools use two kinds of assessment:
- **Formative:** frequent, informal checks during learning, used to decide what to teach next.
- **Summative:** a judgment at the end of a period of teaching, such as an end-of-unit test.

Examples:
- Each Oak lesson has a 6-question **starter quiz** on prior knowledge and a 6-question **exit quiz**.
- In England, Year 6 pupils sit national tests in English reading, grammar, punctuation and spelling, and maths. Writing and science results come from teacher assessment.
- Unit and end-of-year tests are mostly each school's own choice. Our search didn't find a clear official statement for ages 11–14, so verify this.

## Our ladder
| When | What | Where | Rules | Marking | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| During a lesson | Shout it out | TV, together | No score | None | Built |
| Start of a lesson | Starter quiz | Own device | Short, prior knowledge | Automatic | Built |
| End of a lesson | Lesson test | Own device | 12 questions, explanations straight away, retry allowed | Automatic | Built |
| 1, 7, 30 days later | Re-tests | Own device | 4 older questions, past mistakes first | Automatic | Built, basic |
| End of a unit | Unit exam | Tablet, laptop or paper | Adult starts with PIN. Timed, one attempt, answers at the end | Automatic plus parent | Prototype |
| End of term or year | Big exam | Paper or laptop | Mixed, supervised | Automatic plus parent | To build |

The answer to "does the exam come after all chapters?" is: the **lesson test** follows all chapters of one lesson. The **unit exam** follows all lessons in a unit.

## Design rules
- Tests should help learning, so low-stakes questions come often and explain answers.
- Exams look and feel different: paper-style, quiet, timed, no feedback until the end.
- Don't rank children against each other. Compare each child to their own past results.
- A question tests one objective, and is tagged to it, so reports can say what's weak.
- Avoid questions that depend on a third-party video remaining available.
- Levels: the same topic has simpler and harder question sets for different children.
- Fairness: shuffle questions, use separate pools, and supervise big exams. Home tests can't be fully secure, so treat them as learning evidence, not a formal qualification.
- Written answers are checked by the child against the model answer, split into points, one mark each (decided 2026-11-09). No parent marking.

## Question types (like real tests)
Choose one (radio), tick several (checkboxes), dropdown in a sentence (select), write a word, number with a unit, written answer, true/false grid, slider estimate, tap on a diagram, drag labels to a diagram, match pairs with lines, put in order.

## The feeling
- **Lesson test:** dark, playful, one question at a time, instant explanation, sounds. Each right answer adds a bone to a skeleton, so the quiz is itself about the topic. Other subjects should get their own builder, not reuse the skeleton.
- **Exam:** light, paper-like, sections A to D, timer, question map, flag for later, no help until the end.

## Exam parameters in the prototype
30 minutes, 17 questions, 34 marks: 26 automatic and 8 self-checked by the child. Covers all four lessons, though only Lesson 1 has been built. The questions were written from general knowledge and need a subject check.
