# Making tests and exams feel top quality

Date: 2026-11-06. The owner confirmed this brief after rejecting a prototype that copied game themes.

## The brief, in the owner's terms
- Top games are a **quality level** to reach, not something to copy. No towers, monsters, game stories or "Attack!" buttons.
- The test **stays a test,** and the craft goes on the test screens themselves: depth, light, motion and effects.
- **Children do the science.** A question should come from the lesson, and not be plain multiple choice over a picture.
- **Reactions teach.** A right answer makes the muscle really work. A wrong one shows why.
- **Tests feel lively. Exams feel serious.** Same quality, but an exam stays calm, and its big moment is the result.

## What makes it feel top quality
Sources are mostly developer guides, so treat numbers as starting points to tune.

- **Several small effects land together.** One action can combine sound, particles, a brief pause, a flash and a number, all within about 100 ms ([Easton Dev](https://eastondev.com/blog/en/posts/dev/20260521-game-feedback-feel/), [Wayline](https://www.wayline.io/learn/game-feel/1)). The idea comes from the 2012 talk "Juice it or lose it".
- **Scale the effect to what happened.** Big effects on routine actions hide the moments that matter ([game juice checklist](https://gamejuice.pages.dev/articles/game-juice-checklist-30-techniques)).
- **Effects return to rest,** and move the world, not the text being read ([Mega Cat juice guide](https://megacatstudios.com/blogs/game-development/mega-cat-developer-juice-guide-v1-0-08-23-17)).
- **Anticipation and reward animation** are part of why strong games feel satisfying ([Mobile Free to Play on Clash Royale](https://mobilefreetoplay.com/deconstructing-clash-royale/)).
- **My own judgment, unverified:** real materials and light matter as much as motion: reflections, soft glow, shadows and rim light. Direct control also matters, because dragging the thing itself feels better than pressing a button beside it.

## Rewards and learning
- Rewards that reflect real ability can support motivation, while unrelated rewards may reduce it. This is debated, and the classic study is small and old ([summary](https://www.liquisearch.com/overjustification_effect/experimental_evidence)).
- Children aged 8–11 in one math game paid little attention to rewards during play, and competition-based rewards were flagged as a possible risk ([Education Sciences, 2023](https://doaj.org/article/32309d8924824c2bba24c35518693a89)).
- **Our rule:** rewards show real skills from the lesson. No loot boxes, no random prizes, no guilt about streaks, no paying for anything.

## The interaction for each question
| Skill | What the child does | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Contract | Drags a muscle shorter, then says what changed | It really shortens and thickens. Let go early and it relaxes |
| Fuel | Sends things into the blood | Sugar and oxygen light up the cell. Salt and sunlight bounce off |
| Find | Turns a 3D body and taps a place | The heart beats. Any wrong place names the muscle found there |
| Squeeze | Taps a tube to squeeze it | Squeezing behind the food moves it. Squeezing in front blocks it |

## Three looks to compare
Interactions stay the same. Only the look changes.
- **A. Living Lab:** dark glass, glowing tissue, like a science-fiction lab.
- **B. Soft Bright:** bright and glossy, like a premium animated film.
- **C. Show Time:** Home School TV's colors and fonts with dramatic light. My recommendation, because the owner already likes that channel.

## Not known yet
- Whether children learn more this way. It should be tested with the three children.
- Whether older tablets stay smooth. The prototype must be tried on the real devices.
